## Carnivores by design

Human selection has changed how our dogs and cats look, but not how they work inside. Their bodies were perfected over thousands of years of evolution, and domesticated carnivores still need species-appropriate nutrition to thrive.

The signs are easy to read. Dogs and cats have teeth and claws made to catch, rip and tear. Their eyes sit at the front of their heads, letting them focus on prey animals (whose own eyes sit on the sides of the head, to watch for carnivores). Their digestive systems are short and fast, equipped with the enzymes needed for the foods they evolved to eat.

For people and pets alike, eating fewer processed foods and more unadulterated natural ingredients supports better health. The strength of your pet's immune system, their resistance to disease and their quality of life all depend on the type and quality of food they eat. Processed foods are convenient, but they lack vital enzymes and offer a fixed formula with no variety of fresh foods.

## What is raw pet food?

A raw diet is built from muscle meat, organs, whole or ground bone, raw eggs, pet-safe fresh fruits and vegetables, and sometimes dairy such as yogurt. Because nothing is heavily processed, the nutrients stay intact.

Raw food is gaining popularity among pet parents, and for good reason: whether you have a dog or a cat, a nutritious raw diet helps them live a healthier life. Their digestive systems are designed to thrive on raw food, which keeps them energetic, playful and lively. Raw foods are typically protein-rich and ideal for growing or adult dogs — and even a mix of raw and other foods can have tremendous benefits for your pet.

## The benefits of feeding raw {#benefits}

- **Better digestion.** Raw food is naturally rich in enzymes and beneficial bacteria. Many pets have less bloating and firmer stools, with no artificial fillers or excess carbohydrates to strain the gut.
- **Healthier skin and coat.** The essential fatty acids in raw meat and fish mean a shinier coat, less shedding and dander, and fewer skin irritations.
- **Cleaner teeth.** Raw meaty bones encourage natural chewing, which strengthens teeth and gums and reduces tartar. Processed food tends to leave residue behind.
- **Healthy weight and steady energy.** A species-appropriate balance of protein and fat, without high levels of carbohydrates, helps regulate blood sugar and reduce the risk of obesity.
- **Stronger immunity.** Balanced protein, fats, vitamins and minerals support muscles, joints and the immune system — and resistance to disease and parasites.
- **Less odour.** Elimination of foul body odour and bad breath.
- **Strong nails and healthy ears.** Good nutrition shows from nose to tail.

…and the satisfaction of knowing exactly what your pet is eating.

## What's in the bowl {#ingredients}

Understanding the ingredients in your pet's food is crucial. Raw pet food is known for its wholesome, natural components. Here are the key ingredients commonly found in it, and what they do:

**High-quality protein.** Lean meats such as chicken, turkey, beef and fish, for muscle development, energy and overall health.

**Healthy fats.** Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids from sources like fish oil, flaxseed and chicken fat, for skin, coat, joints and cognitive development.

**Natural vitamins and minerals.** Fresh fruits and vegetables supply vitamins A, C, E and B complex, plus calcium, phosphorus, magnesium and potassium for immune function and bone health.

**Enzymes and probiotics.** Naturally occurring enzymes and beneficial microorganisms aid digestion and improve nutrient absorption.

**Whole grains (optional).** Some recipes include brown rice, quinoa or oats for fibre and energy. Not every pet needs grains — especially those with sensitivities or allergies.

By understanding these ingredients, you can make informed choices about your pet's diet and make sure they get a balanced, nutritious meal every time.

## Transitioning to raw {#transitioning}

Switching works best when it's gradual, so your pet's digestive system has time to adjust.

1. **Talk to your veterinarian.** Make sure a raw diet suits your pet's age, health and nutritional needs.
2. **Start with a good appetite.** A half-day or full-day fast before the first raw meal helps.
3. **Introduce it gradually.** Begin with small amounts of raw food and reduce the old food a little at a time.
4. **Watch their digestion.** If you see diarrhea, constipation or an upset stomach, slow down.
5. **Offer variety and balance.** Rotate protein sources, and include fruits, vegetables and supplements, to prevent dietary imbalances.
6. **Keep fresh water available.** Hydration always matters, and especially during a change in diet.

By following these tips and monitoring your pet's response, you can successfully transition to a raw diet and provide optimal nutrition for a happy, healthy life.
